Miete or Mitte? Length Changes Meaning

Walking to school in Lindenberg, Peter asks Lukas: 'Wohnen wir in der Miete or in der Mitte?' Lukas laughs: vowel length turns similar-looking words into different meanings — and German spelling almost always signals which one you need.

Exercise

Which word has a **long** vowel signaled by a silent **h**?

  • wohnen right answer

    Correct! The silent **h** after **o** signals a long *oh*.

  • Wonne

    The double **nn** makes the **o** short.

  • Sonne

    Here too, **nn** signals a short vowel.

A silent **h** before *l, m, n, r* lengthens the vowel (*wohnen, Fahrrad, nehmen*); a double consonant (*nn, tt, mm*) shortens it.

Signals for Long and Short Vowels

Sound-Spelling Board: Signals for long and short vowels
Vowel lengthSpelling signalExamples
Long (stretched)ie (for long ee)Miete, spielen, Biene
Long (stretched)silent h (Dehnungs-h)Fahrrad, wohnen, Lehrer
Long (stretched)double vowel or single consonantKaffee, Boot, Tag, Leben
Short (snappy)double consonant, ck, or tzMitte, Bett, Bäcker, Platz

Never pronounce the silent h!

In Fahrrad, wohnen, or Uhr, the h is completely silent. Its only job is to lengthen the vowel before it.

Practice: Long or Short?

Exercise

Match each word from Peter's school day to its vowel-length signal.

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: Klasse – short a (double consonant ss); Lehrer – long e (silent h); spielen – long i (spelled ie); Bäcker – short ä (ck acts as double k)

On the Sound-Spelling Board, the core rule is: **ie / silent h / double vowel = long**, **double consonant / ck / tz = short**.
