Dots and Arrows on the Sound-Spelling Board

In class, Herr Braun claps out the syllables: LIN-den-berg, FAHR-rad, AR-bei-ten. In native German words, the first syllable (the stem) almost always carries the main stress — slightly louder and clearer than the rest.

Exercise

Where does the main stress fall in the compound noun **Fahrradweg** (*bike path*)?

  • **FAHR**-rad-weg (on the first syllable) right answer

    Correct! In German compounds, the first word (*FAHR-*) carries the main stress.

  • Fahr-**RAD**-weg (on the second syllable)

    The second element carries at most secondary stress, not the main stress.

  • Fahr-rad-**WEG** (on the last syllable)

    In German compounds, main stress pulls to the front word, not the end.

German compounds stress the **first** word because it narrows the meaning: *FAHRradweg*, *DEUTSCHlehrer*, *SCHULhof*.

Word Stress and Sentence Melody at a Glance

Word stress and sentence melody at a glance
AreaPatternExamples
Word stress (default)Stem syllable (usually syllable 1)SCHU-le, WOH-nen, BRÖT-chen
Compound nounsFirst word stressedSCHUL-hof, BAHN-hof, HAUS-aufgabe
StatementVoice falls at the end (↘)Peter wohnt in Lindenberg. ↘
W-questionVoice falls at the end (↘)Wo ist der Bahnhof? ↘
Yes/no questionVoice rises at the end (↗)Kommst du heute mit? ↗

Notice W-questions!

In a neutral W-question (Wie heißt du? ↘), your voice goes down at the end — it rises only if you want to sound extra friendly or surprised.

Practice: Stress and Melody Check

Exercise

Match each sentence from Peter's chat with Nadia to its ending intonation.

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: Hast du morgen Zeit? – rising (↗) – yes/no question; Wann beginnt der Unterricht? – falling (↘) – neutral W-question; Die Schule beginnt um acht Uhr. – falling (↘) – statement

Statements and neutral W-questions end with falling intonation (↘). Yes/no questions end with clear rising intonation (↗).

Exercise

In which words does the **first** syllable carry the main stress?

  • Schulhof right answer

    Correct! **SCHUL**-hof stresses the first word of the compound.

  • Lindenberg right answer

    Correct! **LIN**-den-berg carries main stress on syllable 1.

  • verstehen

    Here *ver-* is an unstressed prefix — the stem is stressed: ver-**STEH**-en.

  • Brötchen right answer

    Correct! **BRÖT**-chen stresses the stem syllable at the front.

Native German words and compounds almost always stress the first syllable — only unstressed prefixes like *ver-* shift stress onto the stem.
