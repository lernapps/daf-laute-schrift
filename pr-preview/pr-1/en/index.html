Sounds and Spelling of German

In Herr Braun's Year 6 class in Lindenberg, Peter builds an interactive Sound-Spelling Board: from letters, umlauts, and consonants through vowel length and capitalization to final devoicing, prefix stress, ss/ß, loanwords, and B1 comma rules.

Contents

  1. Alphabet, Umlauts and Key Consonants

    Peter sets up his Sound-Spelling Board in Year 6 in Lindenberg: spelling aloud, umlauts, diphthongs, and ch, sch, sp, st, and z.

    1. German Alphabet and Spelling Pronounce the German alphabet clearly and spell out names and addresses reliably.
    2. Umlauts and Diphthongs Pronounce German umlauts (ä, ö, ü) with proper lip rounding and distinguish diphthongs (ei, ie, eu/äu, au).
    3. Key Consonant Sounds: ch, sch, sp, st and z Distinguish the ich-sound and ach-sound (ch), and pronounce sch, initial sp/st, and z/tz reliably.
  2. Vowel Length, Word Stress and Capitalization

    Peter extends the Sound-Spelling Board with long/short vowel signals, noun capitalization markers, and word stress and basic intonation.

    1. Long and Short Vowels Hear, pronounce, and spot spelling signals for long vowels (ie, silent h) and short vowels (double consonants).
    2. Capitalization of Nouns and Formal Sie Capitalize sentence beginnings, all German nouns, and the formal pronoun Sie/Ihr reliably.
    3. Word Stress and Basic Intonation Stress the main stem syllable of German words and use natural falling or rising intonation in statements and questions.
  3. Devoicing, Prefix Stress, ss/ß and Basic Commas

    Peter refines the Sound-Spelling Board for A2: final devoicing, r-sounds and unstressed endings, verb-prefix stress, s/ss/ß, and basic commas.

    1. What You Need Here (recap) A quick recap of separable prefix verbs before learning verb-prefix stress.
    2. Final Consonant Devoicing Pronounce final b, d, and g as voiceless p, t, and k and still spell them accurately using the extension test.
    3. R-Sounds and Unstressed Endings Pronounce German initial r-sounds, vocalized -er endings, and reduced unstressed endings (-en, -el) naturally.
    4. Stress on Verb Prefixes Stress separable verb prefixes (aufstehen, einkaufen) clearly and leave inseparable prefixes (verstehen, beginnen) unstressed.
    5. Spelling s, ss, and ß Decide reliably between s, ss, and ß using voicing and the vowel-length test.
    6. Basic Comma Rules Place commas accurately in lists and before subordinate clauses introduced by weil, dass, wenn, or als.
  4. Sentence Emphasis, Loanwords and B1 Spelling

    Peter applies the Sound-Spelling Board to dialogues and the school blog: contrastive stress, loanwords, B1 commas, and compounds & nominalizations.

    1. Sentence Stress and Emphasis Shift sentence stress purposefully to highlight new information or express contrast in conversation.
    2. Pronouncing Common Loanwords Pronounce and stress common international loanwords in German (-tion, -ie, -ieren, -tät, -ik) accurately.
    3. Commas in Complex Sentences Set commas accurately around relative clauses (including embedded ones) and infinitive clauses with um/ohne/statt … zu.
    4. Compounds and Nominalizations Write German compound nouns as single words and capitalize nominalized verbs and adjectives (beim Lesen, alles Gute).
