Why Does Tag Sound Like Tak?

In German class, Peter asks in surprise: 'Lukas says Tak, Kint, and gelp — yet the book writes Tag, Kind, and gelb!' Herr Braun adds a new tile to the Sound-Spelling Board: at the end of a word or syllable, German voiced consonants always turn voiceless.

Final devoicing and the extension test
Spelling → SoundAt word end (voiceless)Extended (voiced audible)
b → [p]gelb, halb, Urlaubgelbe, halbe, Urlaube
d → [t]Kind, Freund, BildKinder, Freunde, Bilder
g → [k]Tag, Weg, ZugTage, Wege, Züge

The extension test for spelling

Unsure whether a word ends in b/p, d/t, or g/k? Extend it (plural or -e): Hund → Hunde (so d), bunt → bunte (so t). In -ig endings (fertig, wichtig), Standard German usually pronounces -ig softly like -ich.

Practice: Hear, Extend, Spell Right

Exercise

Which pair of German words ends with the **exact same sound** despite different spelling?

  • Rad (bike) and Rat (advice) right answer

    Correct! Both sound like *Raat* — only when extended do you hear *Rä**d**er* vs. *Rä**t**e*.

  • Tage and Taten

    Here **g** and **t** stand before a vowel (*-e / -en*), so final devoicing does not apply.

  • gelbe and helle

    In *gelbe*, *b* comes before a vowel and stays voiced.

Final devoicing happens at the end of a word or syllable (*Rad = Rat* spoken), not before a vowel (*Räder ≠ Räte*).

Use the extension test and choose the right final letter.

  1. friend (plural: Freunde) → Freun…
  2. train (plural: Züge) → Zu…
  3. yellow (extended: gelbe) → gel…

Solution
  1. Freund
  2. Zug
  3. gelb
  4. bunt

The extended form reveals the spelling: Freunde → Freund, Züge → Zug, gelbe → gelb, bunte → bunt.
