AUFstehen vs. verSTEHen: Stress Reveals the Grammar

Peter writes two verbs from Herr Braun's class side by side on the Sound-Spelling Board: AUFstehen (get up) and verSTEHen (understand). In German, pronunciation and grammar are directly linked here: if the prefix is stressed, the verb is separable!

Separable vs. inseparable verb prefixes
Prefix typeWhere is the stress?Common prefixes & examples
Separablealways on the prefixab-, an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-: AUFstehen, EINkaufen, ANrufen
Inseparableon the verb stem (prefix unstressed)be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, ver-, zer-: verSTEHen, beGINnen, erZÄHlen

Special case: dual prefixes like um- or über-

A few prefixes (durch-, über-, um-, unter-) even change meaning with stress: UMfahren (knock down, separable) vs. umFAHren (drive around, inseparable); überSETZen (translate, inseparable).

Practice: Stress the Prefix or the Stem?

Exercise

In which verbs does the main stress fall on the **prefix** (separable verb)?

  • einkaufen right answer

    Correct! **EIN**-kaufen has the stressed separable prefix *ein-* (*Ich kaufe ein*).

  • beginnen

    *be-* is always unstressed and inseparable: be-**GIN**-nen.

  • mitkommen right answer

    Correct! **MIT**-kommen stresses the prefix *mit-* (*Kommst du mit?*).

  • erklären

    *er-* is always unstressed and inseparable: er-**KLÄ**-ren.

The eight inseparable prefixes (*be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, miss-, ver-, zer-*) never take main stress; prefixes like *ein-, auf-, an-, mit-* are always stressed.

Exercise

Nadia helps Peter **übersetzen** (*translate*) a sentence into German. Which syllable is stressed?

  • über-**SETZ**-en (on the stem — inseparable) right answer

    Correct! *übersetzen* meaning *to translate* is inseparable: *Nadia übersetzt den Satz.*

  • **Ü**-ber-setzen (on the prefix — separable)

    With stressed *Ü-ber-*, it means *to ferry across a river*, not *to translate*.

Hearing whether the prefix is stressed tells you right away whether the verb splits in a main clause.
