Lehrer or Lehra? Two Faces of German R

Peter listens closely when Mina talks about her Bruder and her Lehrer: the first r in Bruder is a soft consonant in the back of the throat, while the ending -er at the end sounds almost like a short, relaxed -a!

Exercise

In which word does the final **r** sound like a short, relaxed **-a** (vocalized r)?

  • Fenster right answer

    Correct! The unstressed ending **-er** turns into a short a-like sound (*Fensta*) in Standard German.

  • Regen

    In *Regen*, **R** comes at the start before a vowel, so it is a consonant r.

  • fahren

    In *fahren*, the vowel *e* follows *r* directly, so *r* stays consonantal.

Before a vowel (*Regen, fahren*), **r** is a consonant; in the ending **-er** (*Fenster, Lehrer, Vater*), it is vocalized.

Drop the English tongue curl

In English, the tongue tip curls back (car). In German, keep your tongue tip relaxed behind your lower teeth — especially in -er endings (Wasser, Wetter, super).

Exercise

In which words is **r** **vocalized** (sounding like a short *a*) in Standard German?

  • Wetter right answer

    Correct! The unstressed ending *-er* sounds like a short *-a*.

  • hier right answer

    Correct! After long *ie* at syllable end, **r** is vocalized (*hia*).

  • Reise

    Here **R** starts the word before a vowel, so it is consonantal.

  • Vater right answer

    Correct! *-er* in *Vater* is vocalized too.

Vocalized **r** occurs mainly in *-er* endings (*Wetter, Vater*) and after a long vowel at syllable end (*hier, Uhr*).
