Straße, Wasser, Sonne: The Vowel-Length Test for ss vs. ß

On the Lindenberg town map, Sam spots Schlossstraße and Wasserplatz — sometimes ss, sometimes ß! Herr Braun puts the clear decision rule on the Sound-Spelling Board: first check whether s buzzes (voiced) or hisses (voiceless) — and for the sharp hissing s, vowel length decides.

Decision board for s, ss, and ß
SpellingRuleExamples
s (voiced / buzzing)before a vowel at word/syllable start (like English z in zoo)Sonne, Sam, lesen, Reise
ss (sharp / hissing)after a short vowelWasser, Klasse, essen, Fluss, muss
ß (sharp / hissing)after a long vowel or diphthong (ei, au, eu)Straße, Fuß, groß, heißen, draußen

Standard German note

At the end of a word (Haus, Eis), a single s also sounds sharp due to final devoicing, yet stays single s (extended: Häuser, voiced s). And ß never starts a word!

Practice: s, ss, or ß?

Exercise

Decide by vowel length: ss (short vowel) or ß (long vowel / diphthong)?

  1. street (long a) → Stra…e
  2. water (short a) → Wa…er
  3. to be called (diphthong ei) → hei…en
  4. river (short u) → Flu…
Solution
  1. Straße
  2. Wasser
  3. heißen
  4. Fluss

Long vowel or diphthong → ß (Straße, heißen); short vowel → ss (Wasser, Fluss).

Exercise

Why is **groß** spelled with **ß**, while **Klasse** is spelled with **ss**?

  • In *groß* the *o* is long; in *Klasse* the *a* is short. right answer

    Exactly! A sharp voiceless s is spelled **ß** after a long vowel and **ss** after a short vowel.

  • **ß** always goes at the end of a word and **ss** always in the middle.

    Counterexample: *Fluss* and *muss* end in **ss** (short vowel), while *Straße* has **ß** in the middle (long *a*).

  • Nouns always use **ss**, adjectives always use **ß**.

    Part of speech does not matter — *Straße* and *Fuß* are nouns with **ß**, and *nass* is an adjective with **ss**.

The choice between **ss** and **ß** depends solely on the length of the preceding vowel.
