Commas as Clause Signposts in Peter's Email

Peter writes an email home about his week in Lindenberg. In German, commas do not simply mark pauses for breath as they often do in English — they mark clear grammar boundaries: lists and subordinate clauses.

Sound-Spelling Board: The two core A2 comma rules
RuleWhere does the comma go?Examples
1. Listsbetween list items — no comma before und / oderWir kaufen Brötchen**,** Äpfel und Käse.
2. Subordinate clausesalways before (and after) weil, dass, wenn, als, obIch bleibe zu Hause**, weil** es regnet.
Subordinate clause firstcomma right between the two verbsWenn ich Zeit habe**,** spiele ich Fußball.

No Oxford comma in German!

In a simple list, German does not put a comma before und or oder: Lukas, Mina und Peter (never Lukas, Mina, und Peter). Before aber and sondern, however, always use a comma!

Practice: Placing Commas Correctly

Exercise

Which sentence from Peter's email has every comma placed correctly?

  • Ich freue mich, dass ich morgen Nadia, Lukas und Mina treffe. right answer

    Correct! A comma before the *dass*-clause, a comma between *Nadia* and *Lukas*, and no comma before *und*.

  • Ich freue mich dass ich morgen Nadia, Lukas, und Mina treffe.

    This misses the mandatory comma before *dass* and adds an unwanted Oxford comma before *und*.

  • Ich freue mich, dass ich morgen, Nadia Lukas und Mina treffe.

    Do not put a comma after *morgen*; put it between the list items *Nadia* and *Lukas*.

Remember: subordinate clauses (*weil, dass, wenn, als*) **always** take a comma; in lists, *und / oder* replaces the comma.

Exercise

In which sentences is a comma **required** at [ ]?

  • Peter nimmt die Deutsche Bahn [ ] weil sein Fahrrad kaputt ist. right answer

    Correct! *weil* introduces a subordinate clause → comma required.

  • Als Peter in Lindenberg ankam [ ] regnete es stark. right answer

    Correct! After an initial subordinate clause, place a comma before the main-clause verb.

  • Im Rucksack sind ein Heft [ ] und zwei Stifte.

    In a simple list, do not put a comma before *und*.

  • Ich wollte kommen [ ] aber ich hatte Training. right answer

    Correct! Before the contrasting conjunction *aber*, German always requires a comma.

Commas separate subordinate clauses (*weil, dass, wenn, als*) from main clauses and precede *aber* — but not *und* in a list.
