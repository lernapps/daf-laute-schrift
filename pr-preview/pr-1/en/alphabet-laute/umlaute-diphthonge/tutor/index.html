Context: the page “Umlauts and Diphthongs”

Pronounce German umlauts (ä, ö, ü) with proper lip rounding and distinguish diphthongs (ei, ie, eu/äu, au).

Brötchen at the Lindenberg Bakery

In the morning, Sam and Lukas buy fresh Brötchen at the Bäcker. Two small dots change both sound and meaning: Brot becomes Brötchen, schon (already) becomes schön (nice). Herr Braun adds a lip-position tip to the Sound-Spelling Board.

Mouth-position guide for umlauts ä, ö, ü:

  • Umlaut | Mouth tip | Examples
  • ä | open e (like English bed) | Bäcker, spät, März
  • ö | say eh, round your lips as for oh | Brötchen, schön, zwölf
  • ü | say ee, round your lips as for oo | Übung, Schüler, fünf

Diphthongs: Two Vowels, One Glide

In a diphthong, your voice glides from one vowel to the next in one syllable. Watch ei (eye) versus ie (long ee): Wein (wine) and Wien (Vienna) sound completely different!

Diphthongs and ie compared:

  • Spelling | Sound | Examples
  • ei (also ai) | like English eye | eins, zwei, Mai
  • ie (not a diphthong) | long ee (like see) | vier, spielen, Wien
  • eu / äu | like English oy in boy | neu, Deutsch, Häuser
  • au | like English ow in house | Haus, Braun, auch

Remember: Memory trick for ei and ie

The second letter gives the English letter name: ei → English I (eye), ie → English E (ee).

Exercise umlaut-lippen

  • Task: How do you produce German ü in fünf and Schüler?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Tongue as for ee, lips tightly rounded as for oo – Correct! Your tongue stays forward for ee while your lips round tightly for oo.
    • ✗ Exactly like plain u in Schule – Then fünf sounds like funf — u is in the back of the mouth, ü is in the front.
    • ✗ Like a short a with relaxed lips – For ü, your lips must be clearly rounded.
  • Solution: Tongue as for ee, lips tightly rounded as for oo
  • Explanation: All umlauts are articulated at the front: ö combines eh + rounded lips, ü combines ee + rounded lips.

Exercise diphthong-schreiben

  • Task: Choose the matching vowel spelling (ei, ie, eu, äu, or au) for Sam's Lindenberg notes.
  • Answer format: choose a word for each gap
  • How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters wrong, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation wrong
  • Items (start → solution):
  1. four (long ee sound) → vier
  2. two (eye sound) → zwei
  3. German (oy sound) → Deutsch
  4. houses (plural of Haus, oy sound) → Häuser
  • Explanation: ie sounds like long ee (vier), ei like eye (zwei), and eu/äu like oy (Deutsch, Häuser — spelled äu because it comes from Haus).

If the app takes a version of the child's as wrong that you think is right, tell the child: the app knows only the versions above.
