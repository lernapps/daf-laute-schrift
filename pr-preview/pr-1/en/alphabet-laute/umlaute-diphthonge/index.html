Brötchen at the Lindenberg Bakery

In the morning, Peter and Lukas buy fresh Brötchen at the Bäcker. Two small dots change both sound and meaning: Brot becomes Brötchen, schon (already) becomes schön (nice). Herr Braun adds a lip-position tip to the Sound-Spelling Board.

Mouth-position guide for umlauts ä, ö, ü
UmlautMouth tipExamples
äopen e (like English bed)Bäcker, spät, März
ösay eh, round your lips as for ohBrötchen, schön, zwölf
üsay ee, round your lips as for ooÜbung, Schüler, fünf

Diphthongs: Two Vowels, One Glide

In a diphthong, your voice glides from one vowel to the next in one syllable. Watch ei (eye) versus ie (long ee): Wein (wine) and Wien (Vienna) sound completely different!

Diphthongs and ie compared
SpellingSoundExamples
ei (also ai)like English eyeeins, zwei, Mai
ie (not a diphthong)long ee (like see)vier, spielen, Wien
eu / äulike English oy in boyneu, Deutsch, Häuser
aulike English ow in houseHaus, Braun, auch

Memory trick for ei and ie

The second letter gives the English letter name: ei → English I (eye), ie → English E (ee).

Practice: Spotting Umlauts and Diphthongs

Exercise

How do you produce German **ü** in *fünf* and *Schüler*?

  • Tongue as for *ee*, lips tightly rounded as for *oo* right answer

    Correct! Your tongue stays forward for *ee* while your lips round tightly for *oo*.

  • Exactly like plain *u* in *Schule*

    Then *fünf* sounds like *funf* — *u* is in the back of the mouth, *ü* is in the front.

  • Like a short *a* with relaxed lips

    For **ü**, your lips must be clearly rounded.

All umlauts are articulated at the front: **ö** combines *eh* + rounded lips, **ü** combines *ee* + rounded lips.

Exercise

Choose the matching vowel spelling (ei, ie, eu, äu, or au) for Peter's Lindenberg notes.

  1. four (long ee sound) → v…r
  2. two (eye sound) → zw…
  3. German (oy sound) → D…tsch
  4. houses (plural of Haus, oy sound) → H…ser
Solution
  1. vier
  2. zwei
  3. Deutsch
  4. Häuser

ie sounds like long ee (vier), ei like eye (zwei), and eu/äu like oy (Deutsch, Häuser — spelled äu because it comes from Haus).
