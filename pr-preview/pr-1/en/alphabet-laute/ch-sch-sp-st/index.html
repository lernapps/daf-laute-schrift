Ich-Sound or Ach-Sound? Two Sides of ch

Nadia reads Sam a sentence: 'Ich mache heute einen Kuchen in der Küche.' The same ch sounds soft in the front (ich, Küche) and raspy in the back (mache, Kuchen). The letter right before ch decides!

Sound-Spelling Board: ch, sch, sp/st, and z
LettersWhen / How?Examples
ch (ach-sound)after a, o, u, au (back of throat)Buch, Nacht, kochen, auch
ch (ich-sound)after e, i, ä, ö, ü, ei, eu and consonants (front, soft)ich, Milch, Küche, reich
schalways like English sh (lips rounded)Schule, Tisch, Deutsch
sp- / st-at the start of a word or stem: shp / shtSport, Straße, aufstehen
z / tzalways ts (like cats), never English zZug, Zeit, Platz, Katze

Watch out for sp and st at the end!

In the middle or at the end of a word, sp and st stay plain s + p / s + t: Westen, du bist, Fest.

Practice: Consonants on the Sound-Spelling Board

Exercise

In which words is **ch** pronounced as the **ach-sound** (back of the throat)?

  • Buch right answer

    Correct! After **u**, **ch** is always the ach-sound.

  • Bücher

    Because of the umlaut **ü**, **ch** moves forward to the ich-sound!

  • Woche right answer

    Correct! After **o**, **ch** is the ach-sound.

  • Milch

    After a consonant (*l*), **ch** is always the soft ich-sound.

Rule of thumb: after **a, o, u, au**, **ch** is in the back (ach-sound). After every other sound — including *ä, ö, ü* — it is in the front (ich-sound).

Match each Lindenberg word to its consonant sound.

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: Stadtzentrum (St-) – sounds like sht- at the start of a word; Zug (Z-) – sounds like ts- (sharp, like in cats); Fest (-st) – sounds like -st (no sh at word end); Sportverein (Sp-) – sounds like shp- at the start of a word

**sp/st** become *shp/sht* only at the start of a word or stem; at the end (*Fest*), plain *s* remains. **z** is always *ts*.
