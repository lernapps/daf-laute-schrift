Context: the page “Key Consonant Sounds: ch, sch, sp, st and z”

Distinguish the ich-sound and ach-sound (ch), and pronounce sch, initial sp/st, and z/tz reliably.

Ich-Sound or Ach-Sound? Two Sides of ch

Nadia reads Sam a sentence: 'Ich mache heute einen Kuchen in der Küche.' The same ch sounds soft in the front (ich, Küche) and raspy in the back (mache, Kuchen). The letter right before ch decides!

Sound-Spelling Board: ch, sch, sp/st, and z:

  • Letters | When / How? | Examples
  • ch (ach-sound) | after a, o, u, au (back of throat) | Buch, Nacht, kochen, auch
  • ch (ich-sound) | after e, i, ä, ö, ü, ei, eu and consonants (front, soft) | ich, Milch, Küche, reich
  • sch | always like English sh (lips rounded) | Schule, Tisch, Deutsch
  • sp- / st- | at the start of a word or stem: shp / sht | Sport, Straße, aufstehen
  • z / tz | always ts (like cats), never English z | Zug, Zeit, Platz, Katze

Remember: Watch out for sp and st at the end!

In the middle or at the end of a word, sp and st stay plain s + p / s + t: Westen, du bist, Fest.

Exercise ach-laut-finden

  • Task: In which words is ch pronounced as the ach-sound (back of the throat)?
  • Answer format: choose all right answers
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Buch – Correct! After u, ch is always the ach-sound.
    • ✗ Bücher – Because of the umlaut ü, ch moves forward to the ich-sound!
    • ✓ Woche – Correct! After o, ch is the ach-sound.
    • ✗ Milch – After a consonant (l), ch is always the soft ich-sound.
  • Solution: Buch; Woche
  • Explanation: Rule of thumb: after a, o, u, au, ch is in the back (ach-sound). After every other sound — including ä, ö, ü — it is in the front (ich-sound).

Exercise konsonanten-klang

  • Task: Match each Lindenberg word to its consonant sound.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: Stadtzentrum (St-) – sounds like sht- at the start of a word; Zug (Z-) – sounds like ts- (sharp, like in cats); Fest (-st) – sounds like -st (no sh at word end); Sportverein (Sp-) – sounds like shp- at the start of a word
  • Explanation: sp/st become shp/sht only at the start of a word or stem; at the end (Fest), plain s remains. z is always ts.
