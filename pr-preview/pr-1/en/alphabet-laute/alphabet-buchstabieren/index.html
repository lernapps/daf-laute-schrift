Sam's First Day at the Lindenberg Citizens' Office

While registering at the Lindenberg Bürgeramt, the clerk asks: 'Wie schreibt man Ihren Nachnamen?' Sam opens the Sound-Spelling Board from Herr Braun's class: several German letter names sound quite different from English.

Exercise

Sam spells **Weber**: *W – E – B – E – R*. How does the letter **E** sound in the German alphabet?

  • like a long *eh* (as in *say*, without the glide) right answer

    Exactly! German **E** sounds like *eh*, not like English *ee*.

  • like English *ee* (as in *see*)

    That is the German letter **I**, not **E**.

  • like English *eye*

    That is English **I** — in German, **I** sounds like *ee*.

The most common spelling mix-up involves **A** (*ah*), **E** (*eh*), and **I** (*ee*).

Sound-Spelling Board: Tricky Letters in the Alphabet

The German alphabet has 26 basic letters plus ä, ö, ü, and ß (Eszett). Sam's board highlights the letters English speakers mix up most easily:

Key German letter names
LetterName / SoundLindenberg example
A / E / Iah / eh / eeNadia, Weber, Mina
J / V / Wyott (y) / fow (f) / veh (v)Januar, Vogel, Weber
Y / Z / ßYpsilon / tsett (ts) / EszettGymnasium, Zug, Straße

Tip for the phone

On the phone, people often add a keyword: W wie Weber, B wie Berlin, S wie Schule. For capitals, say großes L.

Practice: Spelling Check

Exercise

Match each German letter to its sound when spelling aloud.

Choose the match for each entry.

Right answer: V – fow (usually sounds like f in German words); W – veh (sounds like English v); J – yott (sounds like English y in yes); Z – tsett (sounds like ts in cats)

Keeping V (*fow*), W (*veh*), J (*yott*), and Z (*tsett*) distinct helps you catch German names and addresses on the first try.

Exercise

Frau Weber spells her street name: *W – A – L – D – W – E – G* (*weh – ah – ell – deh – weh – eh – geh*). Which word does Sam write down?

  • Waldweg right answer

    Correct! *veh* is **W**, *ah* is **A**, and *eh* is **E**.

  • Valdvig

    In German, *veh* is **W** (not V) and *eh* is **E** (not I).

  • Woldwag

    *ah* stands for **A**, not O; *eh* stands for **E**.

Using the German letter names (*W = veh, A = ah, E = eh*) lets you write down addresses like *Waldweg* accurately.
