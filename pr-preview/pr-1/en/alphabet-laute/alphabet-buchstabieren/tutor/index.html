Special requirements

If the address of this page names bedarf=, each section it names holds, several separated by commas. It goes before what is said above; if two contradict each other, the stricter one holds. Without bedarf= none holds.

  • kurze-schritte: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, one thought per message, each part of a section with a question of its own. Say how far there is still to go.
  • wenig-lesen: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, without subordinate clauses; say the core of a section yourself instead of having it read. Explain every technical term once, do not correct spelling.
  • mehr-tiefe: Tick off sections the child knows with one checking question. Unlike above you may ask why and what-if questions about the page's examples; but do not make up tasks to work out.

  • rechnen-anschaulich: Show numbers of the page as amounts, e.g. “37 is 3 tens and 7 ones”, but never the result of a task. One step per message, never time pressure.

While registering at the Lindenberg Bürgeramt, the clerk asks: 'Wie schreibt man Ihren Nachnamen?' Sam opens the Sound-Spelling Board from Herr Braun's class: several German letter names sound quite different from English.

Sound-Spelling Board: Tricky Letters in the Alphabet

The German alphabet has 26 basic letters plus ä, ö, ü, and ß (Eszett). Sam's board highlights the letters English speakers mix up most easily:

Key German letter names:

  • Letter | Name / Sound | Lindenberg example
  • A / E / I | ah / eh / ee | Nadia, Weber, Mina
  • J / V / W | yott (y) / fow (f) / veh (v) | Januar, Vogel, Weber
  • Y / Z / ß | Ypsilon / tsett (ts) / Eszett | Gymnasium, Zug, Straße

Remember: Tip for the phone

On the phone, people often add a keyword: W wie Weber, B wie Berlin, S wie Schule. For capitals, say großes L.

Exercise buchstaben-e-i

  • Task: Sam spells Weber: W – E – B – E – R. How does the letter E sound in the German alphabet?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ like a long eh (as in say, without the glide) – Exactly! German E sounds like eh, not like English ee.
    • ✗ like English ee (as in see) – That is the German letter I, not E.
    • ✗ like English eye – That is English I — in German, I sounds like ee.
  • Solution: like a long eh (as in say, without the glide)
  • Explanation: The most common spelling mix-up involves A (ah), E (eh), and I (ee).

Exercise buchstaben-zuordnen

  • Task: Match each German letter to its sound when spelling aloud.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: V – fow (usually sounds like f in German words); W – veh (sounds like English v); J – yott (sounds like English y in yes); Z – tsett (sounds like ts in cats)
  • Explanation: Keeping V (fow), W (veh), J (yott), and Z (tsett) distinct helps you catch German names and addresses on the first try.
  • Link: ../?via=tutor#buchstaben-zuordnen

Exercise strasse-buchstabieren

  • Task: Frau Weber spells her street name: W – A – L – D – W – E – G (weh – ah – ell – deh – weh – eh – geh). Which word does Sam write down?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Waldweg – Correct! veh is W, ah is A, and eh is E.
    • ✗ Valdvig – In German, veh is W (not V) and eh is E (not I).
    • ✗ Woldwag – ah stands for A, not O; eh stands for E.
  • Solution: Waldweg
  • Explanation: Using the German letter names (W = veh, A = ah, E = eh) lets you write down addresses like Waldweg accurately.
