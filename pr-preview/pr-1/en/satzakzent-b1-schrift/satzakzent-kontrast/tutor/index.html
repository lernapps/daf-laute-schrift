Context: the page “Sentence Stress and Emphasis”

Shift sentence stress purposefully to highlight new information or express contrast in conversation.

One Sentence, Three Meanings

While rehearsing for the Lindenberg school podcast, Sam notices: the exact same sentence ('Sam fährt morgen nach Berlin.') answers completely different questions depending on which word gets the sentence stress!

How sentence stress shifts focus:

  • Stressed word in sentence | What is highlighted? | Matching contrast
  • SAM fährt morgen nach Berlin. | the person (not someone else) | not Lukas, but Sam
  • Sam fährt MORGEN nach Berlin. | the time | not on Friday
  • Sam fährt morgen nach BERLIN. | the destination (neutral focus / new info) | not to Hamburg

Remember: Rule of thumb for sentence stress

New or corrected information gets the stress; already known words stay softer and faster. In a neutral sentence, main stress usually falls on the last new content word (often right before a clause-final verb).

Exercise kontrast-korrektur

  • Task: Nadia asks: 'Hast du das Buch gekauft?' Sam wants to correct her: he only borrowed it. Which word should Sam stress?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Nein, ich habe es AUSgeliehen. – Correct! ausgeliehen contrasts with gekauft — and in this separable verb, the prefix AUS- carries the main stress.
    • ✗ Nein, ICH habe es ausgeliehen. – Who has the book is already known — stressing ICH would mean I did, not someone else.
    • ✗ Nein, ich HABE es ausgeliehen. – The auxiliary habe carries no new contrast; the contrast is on the action (ausgeliehen).
  • Solution: Nein, ich habe es AUSgeliehen.
  • Explanation: In conversation, sentence stress moves directly onto the word that delivers the new information or correction.

Exercise frage-fokus-zuordnen

  • Task: Match each question to the answer with the matching sentence stress.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: Wann schreibt ihr den Deutschtest? – Wir schreiben den Test am DIENSTAG.; Wer hat die Kekse mitgebracht? – MINA hat die Kekse mitgebracht.; Fährt Frau Weber mit dem Auto zur Arbeit? – Nein, sie fährt mit dem FAHRRAD.
  • Explanation: The word that directly answers the question or corrects an assumption carries the strongest stress in the sentence.
