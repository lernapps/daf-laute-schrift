Familiar Words, New Rhythm

For the school blog, Peter writes about Information, Musik, fotografieren, and Universität. The words look almost like English — yet on Peter's Sound-Spelling Board, a different rule applies: in loanwords, German stress almost always moves to the end!

Exercise

How is **Information** pronounced in Standard German?

  • In-for-ma-**TSYOHN** (final *-tion* stressed, with *ts*) right answer

    Correct! In German, *-tion* is always pronounced as stressed **-tsyohn**.

  • In-for-**MAY**-shun (as in English)

    That is the English pronunciation — German keeps a clear vowel (*-tsyohn*) and stresses the final syllable.

  • **IN**-for-ma-tion (on the first syllable)

    Unlike native German words, *-tion* loanwords always put main stress on the ending.

Endings such as **-tion**, **-tät**, **-ieren**, and **-ie** reliably pull German main stress to the end of the word.

Common Loanword Endings in German

Common loanword endings in German
EndingStress & soundExamples
-tionstressed -TSYOHN (never English -shun)In-for-ma-TION, Sta-TION, Na-TION
-ierenalways on -IE- (long ee)stu-DIE-ren, fo-to-gra-FIE-ren, te-le-fo-NIE-ren
-tätalways on -TÄT (long ä)U-ni-ver-si-TÄT, Qua-li-TÄT, Ak-ti-vi-TÄT
-ie / -ikusually on the final syllableE-ner-GIE, Bio-lo-GIE, Mu-SIK, Po-li-TIK

Watch out for a few -ik exceptions

Almost all -ik words stress the last syllable (Mu**SIK*, Ma-the-ma-TIK, Fa-BRIK). Only a handful of common words pull stress earlier: TECH-nik, PLAS-tik, Gram-MA-tik.
