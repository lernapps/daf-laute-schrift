Sam is drafting an article for the school blog. At B1, his sentences grow richer: he adds details with relative clauses and states goals with um … zu. The Sound-Spelling Board now adds the B1 comma rules!

B1 comma rules for relative and infinitive clauses
Clause typeComma ruleExample
Relative clause at endalways a comma before itDas ist der Kurs**,** den Sam besucht.
Embedded relative clausetwo commas (like brackets)Der Lehrer**,** der uns hilft**,** heißt Herr Braun.
um / ohne / (an)statt … zucomma is always mandatorySam übt täglich**,** um klarer zu sprechen.
Infinitive with zu after noun/correlatecomma is mandatory (e.g. Zeit, Lust, es)Er hat Lust**,** einen Artikel zu schreiben.

The #1 relative-clause comma trap

With an embedded relative clause, never forget the closing comma right after the relative clause's verb: Das Fahrrad*,** das im Hof steht**,** gehört Lukas.*

Practice: School Blog Comma Check

Exercise

Which sentence places the commas around the embedded relative clause correctly?

  • Die Schülerin, die neben Sam sitzt, heißt Nadia. right answer

    Correct! The relative clause *die neben Sam sitzt* is framed by two commas like brackets.

  • Die Schülerin, die neben Sam sitzt heißt Nadia.

    The closing comma between the two verbs *sitzt* and *heißt* is missing.

  • Die Schülerin die neben Sam sitzt, heißt Nadia.

    In German, every relative clause needs an opening comma too (unlike restrictive relative clauses in English).

Unlike English, German sets **every** relative clause off with commas — when embedded mid-sentence, it needs **two** commas.

Exercise

In which sentences is a comma **mandatory** at [ ]?

  • Sam geht in die Bibliothek [ ] um in Ruhe zu lernen. right answer

    Correct! Infinitive clauses with *um … zu* always take a comma.

  • Lukas ging los [ ] ohne seine Jacke mitzunehmen. right answer

    Correct! *ohne … zu* and *(an)statt … zu* always require a comma.

  • Mina hat keine Zeit [ ] heute länger zu bleiben. right answer

    Correct! The infinitive phrase depends on the noun *Zeit* → comma is mandatory.

  • Sam lernt [ ] Deutsch.

    This is a simple main clause with no subordinate or infinitive clause — no comma here.

Always use a comma before **um / ohne / (an)statt … zu** and before infinitive phrases depending on a noun (*Zeit, Lust, Plan*) or correlate (*es, daran*).
